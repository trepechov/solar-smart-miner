# feat: a temperature reading counts only once the chips have recovered; then a 2,700 W stress test

## Summary

The owner asked for a 2,700 W power step above today's 2,500 W top. **The purpose is stability, not
yield**: we are still developing the integration, and pushing the miners to their limit is how we
find out whether the temperature rules and their workflows really work. Doing it now, on cool
autumn days, is the safe time; in summer, with sun to spare and a hot room, a failure costs far more.
Efficiency doesn't matter here: the miners only use surplus solar that would otherwise be held back,
so even a much worse J/TH at the top step is a gain.

The research (farm history 2026-10-09 to 16) found one weakness that matters at any top step and
that 2,700 W would make worse: **the band decides on temperature readings taken while the chips are
still cooled by the miner's own restart.** That is what caused the 2026-10-11 loop (below), and it
is still open for every change the 15-minute hold-down doesn't cover. So:

- **P0** the owner checks the hardware (firmware limits, circuits, rated power).
- **P1** (the only code change): a miner's temperature counts for a step-up only once its chips have
  recovered from its own restart. One condition for every restart; it replaces the thermal part of
  the hold-down. The band itself stays the headroom check. Rule count unchanged or lower.
- **P2** the knowledge base gets the measurements (recovery time, per-step rise, the day's drift,
  the firmware cutoff that isn't 80 deg C).
- **P3** the owner adds 2,700 W as a setting and runs a stress test **with the band lowered**, so the
  step-down path really fires. It hasn't fired on the farm since the 10-11 fix.

The default power steps don't change: 2,700 W is the reference farm's choice, not a default.

## What the farm data shows

### The loop on 2026-10-11 (0.8.1)

Every limit change, with the hottest chip at that moment:

```
11:34 Brod3 2500→2300  chip 74   too warm, step down
11:39 Brod3 2300→2500  chip 59   5 min later, chips cooled by the restart → raised again
11:46 Brod3 2500→2300  chip 74
11:52 Brod3 2300→2500  chip 53
 … every ~12 min until 12:47 (9 rounds), then Brod1 from 12:52 to 15:05 (10 rounds)
```

The miner wasn't cooler at 2,300 W (it ran at about 71 to 72 deg C there). The restart alone dropped
the chips 15 to 20 deg C, and the band read that as headroom: 37 of the day's 74 limit changes.
Commit 6271e29 (that night) added the 15-minute hold-down after a step down by Safety or Limits.

**Since then (10-12 to 10-16) the band has not stepped a miner down once**: every limit decrease
was an energy cut, the hottest chips stayed at or below the step-down point (76 deg C on 10-12,
67 to 72 on 10-15 and 10-16; 10-13 and 10-14 were cloudy). The hold-down has been tested by tests and
replays only. And the same cold read still happens after a miner's own *energy* change, which the
hold-down doesn't cover: 5 step-ups to 2,300 or 2,500 W were decided 3 to 8 minutes after that
miner's own change, on chips at 47 to 61 deg C (they settled at 61 to 70).

### How fast the chips recover after a raise (2026-10-15, 10-second recorder data)

| Raise | Draws the new limit | Chips within 2 deg C of settled | Rise flat (< 1 deg C in 5 min) | Hashrate settled | Settled chip |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Brod1 10:32, 2100→2500 | 3.2 min | 5.3 min | 10.5 min | 7.8 min | 64 |
| Brod2 10:29, 2100→2500 | 2.8 min | 5.0 min | 10.5 min | 8.3 min | 62 |
| Brod3 10:47, 2300→2500 | 3.2 min | 5.2 min | ~10 min | 8.8 min | 64 |
| Brod3 14:00, 2300→2500 | 3.8 min | 6.0 min | ~10 min | 9.7 min | 70 |
| Brod1 15:51, 900→1100 | 1.2 min | 1.7 min | **38.5 min** | 7.8 min | 47 |

- The restart (about +1 min) drops the chips to 39 to 42 deg C. The miner draws its new limit at +3 to
  4 min (overshooting to about 2,600 W for a minute), and **the ramp lock ends there, with chips
  at 44 to 54 deg C**. They are within 2 deg C of settled at +5 to 6 min. Confirming that the rise
  has stopped takes about 10 min.
- Hashrate settles *after* the chips (8 to 10 min) and is noisy (about ±10 TH/s minute to minute,
  one second dip at +13 to 21 min), so it is no signal for temperature.
- **A trend window must start at the restart's low point.** A 5-minute window across the restart
  compares 61 deg C before it with 61 after the warm-up and calls it flat at +5.0 min.
- **Far below the target the trend means nothing.** At 1,100 W a cold miner rose slowly from 34 to
  47 deg C for 38 minutes; a pure trend rule would have blocked it the whole time, 20 deg C below
  the target.

### What moves a settled temperature later (2026-10-15, all three at 2,500 W from 11:00)

| | 11:00 | 12:00 | 13:00 | 13:30 | 14:20 | 15:00 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Brod1 chip | 64 | 65 | 67 | 68 | 67 | 67 |
| Brod2 chip | 62 | 64 | 66 | 66 | 66 | 66 |
| Brod3 chip | 64 | 65 | 70 | 70 | 70 | (stepped down) |
| Inverter internal (room proxy) | 33.5 | 36.7 | 39.1 | 40.3 | 39.8 | 38.5 |

- **The day** (room and tank): +4 to 7 deg C over 2.5 h at constant power, about 1.5 to 3 deg C per
  hour, while the outdoor temperature stayed at 13 to 15 deg C. The farm has no room sensor.
- **The other miners**: Brod3 dropping 800 W moved Brod1 by about 1 deg C, dropping 1,600 W for 30 min
  didn't move the others at all (`farm.temperature-coupling`).
- **One step**: 2,300→2,500 W added +3 deg C, 2,100→2,500 W +6 to 7, so about 3 deg C per 200 W in a
  cool tank (the earlier estimate was 5 to 6).

Both are slow and small per event, so no rule can see them coming. The band's headroom has to
absorb them: a miner steps up only below the target, so the tolerance (10 deg C on the farm) must
cover one step, plus the neighbours, plus the afternoon drift. On a warm day that sum can exceed
it, and the band steps the miner down in the afternoon. That is the right answer to a warmer room,
and it doesn't loop: going back up needs a reading 10 deg C below the step-down point. If the lower
step also gets too warm, the band steps down again, one step and one restart at a time (10-11:
Brod3 2500 → 2300 → 2100 → 1900 from 13:02 to 13:17).

### At 2,700 W (estimate)

Settled hottest chip at 2,500 W measured, 2,700 W at +3 to 6 deg C per 200 W:

| Outdoor | At 2,500 W | At 2,700 W |
|---|---|---|
| ~13 deg C (10-15, 10-16) | 62 to 70 | 65 to 76 |
| ~21 deg C (10-12) | 69 to 73 | 72 to 79 |
| ~24 deg C (10-10, 10-11) | 71 to 80 (peak 82) | 74 to 86 |

With the farm band (target 67, step down at 77) and a recovered reading, a cool day settles
2,700 W below the step-down point, so **the step-down would rarely fire on its own**: that's why P3
lowers the band. Miner 3 runs 3 to 5 deg C hotter and draws 30 to 85 W over its limit.

## P0: the owner checks the farm (no code)

1. **The firmware's real temperature limits** on each miner (hot / dangerous / shutdown, chip or
   board). On 2026-10-10 miner 3 ran at 80 to 82 deg C for over 30 minutes at 2,500 W without cutting
   out, so the knowledge base's "about 80 deg C" is wrong. The figure goes into P2.
2. **The circuits.** At 2,700 W a miner draws about 2,730 to 2,790 W, about 12.5 to 13 A at 210 to
   220 V, right at 80 % of a 16 A circuit; the fleet plus the house about 8.8 kW.
3. **The miner's rated power.** 2,700 W must be at or below what the model and its power supply are
   rated for (hass-miner allows 500 to 3,500 W, which says nothing about the hardware).

A "no" on 2 or 3 drops P3 only; P1 and P2 are worth doing for 2,500 W.

## P1: a temperature reading counts only once the chips have recovered (code)

**Change `rule.temperature-band`, don't add a rule.**

- After a change on a miner (sent or seen: a step, a start, a restart the firmware or the owner
  caused), its chips are **recovering** until their rise has stopped: measured from the lowest
  reading since that change, the hottest chip rose less than about 1 deg C over the last 5 minutes.
  On the reference farm that is about 10 minutes after a raise.
- A recovering miner **may not step up** if it was near the target before the change: its last
  recovered reading before the change was within the tolerance below the target (farm: 57 deg C
  or more). It is capped at its current step, like a miner inside the band. A miner that was far
  below the target steps up freely while it recovers (the cold morning case).
- Everything else stays: the band steps a miner down on any reading at or above the step-down point
  (a high reading is never a false alarm); a cut for the import may take it; a stopped miner may
  start; other miners may step up, so the increment goes to the next miner (of equal miners it
  already picks the coolest, which is usually the one that just restarted; the trace now says why it
  was skipped).
- **The band stays the headroom check**: step up only below the target. No predicted rise per step,
  no memory of the day's steps (owner, 2026-10-16). The trace names the headroom: "Brod1 66 deg C,
  11 deg C below the step-down → may step up" / "Brod3 recovering from its restart (was 72 deg C)
  → no step up".
- **One notion of each thing.** The ramp lock (pacing) stays the one notion of "settled after a
  change" for power: the miner draws its new limit. "Recovered" is a property of the temperature
  reading, used only by the band. The 15-minute hold-down after a **too-warm** step down is retired:
  the recovery condition covers it (stepped down at 75 deg C, the miner was near the target, so it
  holds until its chips have recovered and then reads about 71, inside the band). The hold-down
  stays for low voltage and battery (`HELD_DOWN_REASONS` loses `REASON_TOO_WARM`), and with it
  `CONF_HOLD_DOWN`. **Rule count: unchanged** (the band gains a clause, the hold-down loses a case).
- With no readings since the change (after an HA restart, a miner just come back), the miner counts
  as recovering only if it was near the target; with no reading before the change either, it counts
  as near (the safe side).

Code (planning detail, to check against the code when implementing):

- `control.py`: `Controller.note_change` keeps each miner's last change time in a dict that outlives
  its Settling.
- `coordinator.py`: a short per-miner history of the hottest chip (about 15 minutes, one point per
  update), the reading before each change, and a `_recovering()` next to `_held_down`, passed to
  `build_decision` and into the decision log inputs (replays see it).
- `decision/__init__.py`, `context.py`: the new input on the Context.
- `decision/limits.py`: in the band loop, a running miner in `ctx.recovering` gets its cap and the
  trace line; the too-warm check before it is unchanged.
- `decision/rules.py`: `HELD_DOWN_REASONS` without `REASON_TOO_WARM`.
- The threshold (1 deg C in 5 minutes) is a constant unless the tests show it depends on the cooling;
  then it becomes a setting with the reference farm's value as default.

Tests (same commit):

- `tests/test_decision.py`: a recovering miner that was near the target isn't raised, the next one
  is; a recovering miner that was far below is raised; a recovering miner at or above the step-down
  point steps down; a stopped miner may start; once recovered, a reading inside the band holds; with
  every running miner recovering and near the target, nothing steps up and the trace says why.
- `tests/test_coordinator.py`: recovery measured from the low point after a change (not across the
  restart: the 61 → 40 → 61 case of 10-15 is not "flat at 5 minutes"); the reading before the change
  kept; recovery after an HA restart with no history.
- `tests/test_replay.py`: moments from 2026-10-11 (11:39 and 12:05, Brod3 raised 5 minutes after a
  too-warm step down, at 59 and 55 deg C) and 2026-10-16 10:17 (Brod2 raised 2,300 → 2,500 W 4 minutes
  after its own change, at 47 deg C): the raise doesn't happen. Built from the recorder history

- `tests/test_farm_sim.py`: the farm model gets a chip temperature (a restart drops it about 20 deg C,
  it recovers in about 5 minutes, settles at a temperature per step plus a slow drift). Then: a
  clear day with the band set so the top step overheats ends with no flip-flop (no up-down pair on
  one miner within 20 minutes); a clear morning ramp from 900 W still reaches the top, at most about
  15 minutes later than before.

## P3: the 2,700 W stress test (owner, no code)

Only after P0 and the P1 release.

1. **On a cool, clear day**, Configure, power steps
   `900, 1100, 1300, 1500, 1700, 1900, 2100, 2300, 2500, 2700`. Control mode **Manual** for the first
   step of each miner to 2,700 W: the first run is a new tuning (about 50 minutes, poor efficiency,
   hashrate dips, `farm.tuning-duration`), so don't read anything until it ends. Then Automatic.
2. **Lower the band** for the test day so the step-down really fires: for example target 60, step
   down at 70 deg C. This exercises the recovery condition, the hold and the step-down at 2,500 to
   2,700 W while the chips stay well below any firmware limit. Restore the farm band (67 / 77)
   afterwards.
3. **Record:** each temperature step-down and what followed (the next change on that miner and its
   recovered reading), any up-down pair on one miner within 20 minutes (a flip-flop: a bug), the
   recovery time after each change, the settled chip at 2,700 W, draw, TH/s, J/TH, voltage.
4. **Stop rule:** any chip at 80 deg C or more, a low-voltage stop or a tripped breaker → remove
   2,700 from the steps and record why in the reference farm file.
5. Results go to `farm.settled-values`, `farm.temperature-per-step` and
   `farm.temperature-after-restart`. A second day at the normal band shows how often the afternoon
   drift steps a miner down from the top by itself.

## Not in scope

- The default power steps.
- A room-temperature sensor or a forecast-based lookahead: deferred until P3 shows the tolerance
  isn't enough.
- A predicted rise per step or a memory of the day's steps (considered, owner chose the fixed band).
- A fourth miner instead of a higher top step.

## Open questions

- **Two steps at sunrise into the top.** A 2-step increment is about +6 to 12 deg C, against the band's
  rule that the tolerance cover one move. Sunrise is the coolest time, so it should land inside the
  band; P3 checks it. If it doesn't, try a smaller transition step setting before any special case.
- **Recovery after a step down.** The 10-15 data is all raises; a step down (the chips fall and come
  back to a lower settled value) is measured only from 10-11. P3 confirms the same threshold works.
